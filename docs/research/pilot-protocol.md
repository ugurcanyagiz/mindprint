# MINDPRINT Pilot Protocol

## Status and purpose

This is a planning protocol for an experimental cognitive assessment. It does
not establish that MINDPRINT is validated, standardized, diagnostic,
population-normed, or equivalent to an IQ measure.

The purpose of the pilot program is to determine whether the experimental task
families produce interpretable, usable, and psychometrically promising
behavioral measurements before any expansion of the production short form.

## Phase A — cognitive interview / usability pilot

Planning target: approximately 10–20 participants.

This phase is qualitative and usability-focused. It is not a psychometric
validation sample.

Primary questions:

- Is task wording understood as intended?
- Are there unintended clues or obvious shortcuts?
- Do participants describe reasoning processes compatible with the intended
  construct target?
- Are instructions, multi-phase transitions, confidence judgments, and dense
  evidence-selection interactions understandable?
- Are there mobile, keyboard, accessibility, or layout problems?
- Are there unexpected cultural or language interpretations?

A task may be revised after this phase. Revisions require a version increment
before quantitative data are pooled with earlier forms.

## Phase B — initial quantitative pilot

Planning target: approximately 150–300 completed participants.

This is a planning range, not a claim that the range is sufficient for every
psychometric model.

Primary descriptive goals:

- completion and abandonment patterns;
- missingness by task and phase;
- objective response distributions;
- confidence distributions and calibration gaps;
- floor and ceiling patterns;
- response-duration distributions;
- revision behavior;
- preliminary item–remainder associations;
- redundancy between tasks;
- exploratory dimensional patterns.

No task should be promoted to the production short form solely because it
performs well on one descriptive statistic.

## Phase C — larger validation sample

A larger sample, potentially 500+ completed participants, is a future planning
reference rather than a fixed minimum.

Actual sample requirements must be determined from:

- the number of parameters;
- selected factor or IRT model;
- number and type of items;
- language groups;
- DIF/fairness comparisons;
- desired precision;
- power or simulation results.

## Participant experience

A future pilot flow should clearly separate research participation from the
public production experience.

Participants should receive:

1. study information and consent;
2. approximate duration;
3. disclosure that the assessment is experimental and non-clinical;
4. the experimental battery;
5. a neutral completion screen;
6. no percentile, IQ-equivalent, diagnostic, or validated-trait claim.

## Consent and privacy minimums

Before real research collection begins, consent text must explain:

- participation is voluntary;
- the study is experimental and not standardized or clinical;
- which responses and process data are stored;
- whether response timing is stored;
- confidence responses and revisions;
- assessment language;
- whether device/browser class is collected;
- which demographics are optional;
- retention period;
- deletion or withdrawal process where applicable;
- study purpose and contact route.

Data minimization is preferred. Use age bands instead of exact birth dates when
exact age is not required. Do not collect sensitive personal information merely
because it may be analytically interesting.

## Candidate participant context

Potential research context includes:

- pseudonymous participant ID;
- study version;
- assessment language;
- optional age band;
- optional education band;
- optional primary language;
- optional digital-use frequency;
- optional AI-use frequency;
- device class;
- consent version.

These fields are not currently collected by the product.

## Completion definition

A completed participant has reached the defined end of the assigned study form.
Task-level statuses remain distinct:

- completed;
- skipped;
- abandoned;
- incomplete phase;
- interrupted.

Missing research responses must not automatically be converted into incorrect
answers.

## Test–retest planning

A future test–retest phase may use an interval of roughly 1–3 weeks as a
starting planning range. The interval is not a universal psychometric rule.

Fixed-answer tasks are vulnerable to memory and practice effects. Parallel
forms should be developed before strong stability claims are made.

## Before recruitment

Real pilot recruitment should not begin until all of the following exist:

- final study and consent text;
- privacy and retention policy;
- deletion/withdrawal process;
- participant eligibility rules;
- locked task versions;
- locked analysis plan;
- secure research storage and access controls;
- ethics/IRB review determination where applicable to the actual study context.
