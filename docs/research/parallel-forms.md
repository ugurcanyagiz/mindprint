# MINDPRINT Parallel Forms

## Purpose

Milestone 15 introduces six parallel-form candidates:

- Dynamic Attention Form A / Form B
- Hidden-System Learning Form A / Form B
- Dynamic Evidence Stream Form A / Form B

These forms are candidates for future reliability and practice-effect research.

They are not assumed equivalent.

## Structural equivalence

Forms inside each family share a structuralTemplateId and match on key design
properties.

### Attention

Both forms have:

- eight signals;
- two diagnostic signals;
- six irrelevant signals;
- one salient distractor;
- one interruption;
- four event frames;
- final selection plus confidence.

Surface domains differ: digital service vs fulfillment operations.

### Hidden System

Both forms have:

- initial stable learning;
- one anomaly;
- a return to initial stability;
- one true change point;
- three post-change trials;
- one transfer probe;
- limited explicit rule-hypothesis checkpoints.

The mathematical surface rules differ.

### Evidence Stream

Both forms have:

- four evidence events;
- supportive and contradictory directions;
- weak/moderate/strong evidence;
- one strong contradiction;
- judgment and confidence at each checkpoint.

Surface domains differ: learning intervention vs operational routing.

## Counterbalancing

assignFormOrder(sessionId, studyVersion) creates deterministic A/B ordering for
future repeated-form studies.

Form assignment is reproducible for auditing and debugging.

## Practice effects

Hidden-system and evidence-stream forms can be particularly vulnerable to
memory for fixed content.

Alternate forms reduce but do not eliminate practice effects.

Future test-retest studies should examine:

- order effects;
- carryover;
- learning from the first administration;
- form-by-time interactions.

## Equivalence questions

Future pilot analysis should compare:

- completion;
- outcome distributions;
- trace distributions;
- confidence;
- missingness;
- form order;
- device effects.

Only empirical evidence can support stronger parallel-form equivalence claims.
